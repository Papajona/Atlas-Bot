# Atlas 3.10.35 — Strategy Fact Check & Upgrade

## What Atlas can legitimately claim

### 1. Trend following / time-series momentum
This is the strongest research foundation in the current strategy suite. Atlas uses a causal trend signal, time-series momentum and price-channel breakout components. Published research has documented time-series momentum across multiple liquid markets and long horizons.

Sources:
- Moskowitz, Tobias J.; Ooi, Yao Hua; Pedersen, Lasse H. (2012), **Time Series Momentum**, *Journal of Financial Economics*, 104(2), 228–250.
- Hurst, Brian; Ooi, Yao Hua; Pedersen, Lasse H. (2017), **A Century of Evidence on Trend-Following Investing**, *The Journal of Portfolio Management*.

These sources do **not** establish that any particular Atlas parameter set will be profitable in the future.

### 2. ATR / RSI / ADX
These are valid technical-analysis calculations associated with J. Welles Wilder's work. Atlas now treats them as volatility/context/confirmation features rather than proof of alpha.

Source:
- Wilder, J. Welles (1978), **New Concepts in Technical Trading Systems**.

### 3. MACD
MACD is a widely used trend/momentum indicator. Atlas uses the histogram as a normalized confirmation feature. It is not described as an independently proven alpha source.

### 4. VWAP
VWAP is primarily an execution/benchmark concept. Atlas no longer fabricates unit volume when volume is absent, and it no longer gives VWAP a default directional-alpha weight in the ensemble. It is reported as a context feature when usable volume is supplied.

### 5. Mean reversion
The z-score construction is mathematically standard, but its trading performance is context-dependent. Atlas keeps it behind a trend-strength filter and requires the same forward validation as the other components.

### 6. Session opening ranges
Opening ranges are research features, not established universal alpha rules. Atlas now calculates London and New York first-hour ranges using local timezone rules and only exposes the range after that hour has completed.

## Backtest integrity upgrades

- Strategy Lab candidates are now generated by the real AI provider and bound to a supported Atlas strategy family (trend, momentum, breakout, mean reversion or ensemble) before validation. The app no longer backtests a generic ensemble while presenting it as proof for an arbitrary AI-generated strategy.

- Higher-timeframe mappings now apply a conservative source-bar lag, removing the prior mismatch between comment and implementation.
- Opening-range calculations no longer leak the full session's high/low into bars that occurred before the range was complete.
- Research cost turnover is aligned to next-bar execution timing.
- Protected component backtest returns and drawdowns are now calculated from the same protected equity series used for those reported metrics.
- Paper-candidate promotion now requires both historical gates and per-strategy forward/OOS evidence.
- A negative active-bar return is no longer mislabeled as a precise stop-loss event.
- The standard strategy backtest explicitly reports that it is a signal/return backtest; intrabar stop/target behavior is tested by the execution-aware gated backtest.
- Annualization differs for crypto (24/7) versus forex/commodity research rather than treating all markets as identical calendars.

## Research-snooping control

Atlas records a multiple-testing screening proxy and keeps its language explicit: it is **not** a formal Deflated Sharpe Ratio. Research ordering is an internal screening order, not an investment recommendation.

A formal reference for data-snooping concerns is:
- White, Halbert (2000), **A Reality Check for Data Snooping**, *Econometrica*, 68(5), 1097–1126.

## Status

The strategy suite is suitable for research and paper/shadow testing. No result in this document should be interpreted as a forecast or guarantee of future returns.
